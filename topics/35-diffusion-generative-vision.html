Diffusion Models & Generative Vision

Generative models that learn to reverse a noising process step by step — the architecture behind modern text-to-image, video, and 3D synthesis systems.

Foundation

What Is It?

A diffusion model generates data by learning to undo the gradual destruction of data. The forward process is fixed and requires no learning: take a clean image and add small amounts of Gaussian noise over many steps until nothing remains but noise. The model's only job is to learn the reverse — given a noisy image and a timestep, predict the noise that was added — and once it can do that, you can start from pure noise and denoise your way to a novel sample.

This reframing is what made the approach practical. Ho et al. (2020) showed with DDPM that training reduces to a simple noise-prediction regression loss, a weighted variant of the variational bound, and that this alone produces high-fidelity images. Song et al. (2021) then unified the picture: diffusion is a discretisation of a stochastic differential equation, and the network is estimating the score — the gradient of the log data density — which connects denoising diffusion, score matching, and continuous-time generative modelling into one framework with a shared toolbox of numerical solvers.

Simple Analogy

Imagine watching a drop of ink disperse in water until the glass is uniformly grey, and filming it. Training a diffusion model is like teaching someone to run that film backwards one frame at a time: from any cloudy frame, predict what the slightly less cloudy frame looked like. Once they can do that reliably, hand them a glass of plain grey water and they can run it backwards all the way to a drop of ink that was never there.

Diffusion displaced GANs as the dominant approach to image generation for reasons that are largely about training behaviour rather than peak sample quality. There is no adversarial game to balance, no mode collapse, and the objective is a stable regression that scales predictably with data and compute. The cost is sampling: a GAN needs one forward pass, while a naive diffusion sampler needs hundreds, and most of the engineering progress in the field has been about paying that bill down.

Motivation

Why It Matters

Within a few years diffusion went from a research curiosity to the substrate of a creative industry, and simultaneously became a general-purpose tool for any problem that can be cast as sampling from a conditional distribution over continuous data.

  • 🎨 Controllable high-fidelity synthesis: Text, sketches, depth maps, poses, and masks can all condition generation, giving practitioners fine-grained control that was never achievable with earlier generative families.
  • 📐 A principled probabilistic footing: The score-based SDE view supplies exact likelihood computation, well-understood solvers, and a clean account of what the model is estimating — a rarity among deep generative methods.
  • 🧬 Reach far beyond images: The same machinery drives audio and music generation, video, molecular and protein structure design, and robot trajectory planning, because all of these are continuous-domain sampling problems.
  • 🗂️ Synthetic data at scale: Conditional diffusion produces labelled training data for perception tasks and augments long-tail classes where real examples are scarce, expensive, or legally restricted.
  • 🌍 Foundation for world simulation: Video diffusion models trained at scale acquire useful approximations of physics and object permanence, positioning them as learned simulators for embodied agents.
Mechanism

How It Works

Training and sampling are almost mirror images. Training corrupts real data and learns to predict the corruption; sampling starts from corruption and iteratively removes it. Almost every design decision in the field is about which space to do this in and how few steps it can be done in.

Forward noising: A fixed variance schedule defines how much noise is present at each timestep, and a convenient closed form lets you jump straight to any timestep in one operation rather than simulating the chain. Training samples a random timestep, corrupts the example accordingly, and asks the network to predict the added noise. The schedule matters more than it looks: how noise levels are spaced and how the loss is weighted across them strongly affects which frequencies the model learns to get right.

Learned reverse process: The denoiser is conditioned on the timestep and on whatever conditioning signal is used, and it is called repeatedly by a sampler. DDIM reformulates sampling as a deterministic process, which both cuts the number of steps needed by roughly an order of magnitude and gives a reversible mapping between noise and image that makes editing and interpolation possible. Latent diffusion (Rombach et al., 2022) moves the entire process into the compressed latent space of a pretrained autoencoder, cutting the spatial resolution the denoiser must handle by a large factor — the change that made Stable Diffusion trainable and runnable on consumer hardware.

Guidance and conditioning: Classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2022) trains one network to handle both conditional and unconditional prediction by randomly dropping the condition, then at sampling time extrapolates away from the unconditional prediction. A single guidance scale then trades diversity for prompt adherence: low values give varied but loosely matched samples, high values give sharp, on-prompt, and increasingly homogeneous ones, with visible saturation artefacts at the extreme. Nearly every deployed text-to-image system relies on this one trick.

Techniques

Key Techniques

Latent Diffusion

Run diffusion in a perceptually compressed autoencoder latent instead of pixels. Reduces compute per step dramatically while preserving quality, at the cost of an autoencoder whose reconstruction limits become the model's ceiling on fine detail and text rendering.

Diffusion Transformers

Peebles & Xie (2023) replaced the U-Net with a transformer over latent patches, showing quality improves smoothly with model FLOPs. This shifted the field toward transformer backbones and made diffusion inherit the scaling playbook developed for language models.

Flow Matching & Rectified Flow

Reformulates generation as learning a velocity field that transports noise to data along near-straight paths, trained by regressing to a conditional target. Simpler to state than the SDE view, empirically robust, and straighter paths mean fewer solver steps.

Consistency & Step Distillation

Consistency models and progressive or adversarial distillation train a student to map any point on a sampling trajectory directly to its endpoint, collapsing hundreds of steps into one to four. This is what makes real-time and interactive generation feasible.

Structural Control

ControlNet (Zhang et al., 2023) clones and freezes the backbone, adding a trainable branch driven by edge maps, depth, segmentation, or pose. Related adapter methods add conditioning or style without retraining, making control a cheap, composable add-on.

Video & 3D Diffusion

Extends denoising over space and time with spatiotemporal attention for video, or optimises a 3D representation through score distillation from a 2D model. Both push against the field's hardest constraints: temporal coherence and multi-view consistency.

Challenges

Challenges

🐌

Sampling Cost

Iterative refinement is inherently expensive, and aggressive distillation to very few steps still tends to cost diversity and fine detail. Video multiplies the problem by the frame count, keeping high-resolution generation firmly in datacenter territory.

📏

Broken Evaluation

FID depends on a dated Inception feature space, is biased by sample count, correlates poorly with human judgement at high quality, and says nothing about prompt faithfulness or compositional correctness. The field lacks a metric it trusts.

🔍

Memorisation & Copyright

Duplicated training examples can be reproduced near-verbatim, as extraction studies have demonstrated. Combined with web-scraped datasets and style imitation, this creates unresolved legal exposure and drives demand for provenance and watermarking.

🧩

Compositional Reasoning

Models still mishandle counting, spatial relations, attribute binding across multiple objects, and negation. Guidance sharpens images without fixing the underlying failure to represent structured relationships between the things being drawn.

Literature

Key Papers & Resources

  • 📄
    Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models
    Ho, Jain & Abbeel, 2020 — Reduces diffusion training to a simple noise-prediction objective and demonstrates image quality competitive with the best GANs of the time.
  • 📄
    Score-Based Generative Modeling through Stochastic Differential Equations
    Song et al., 2021 — Unifies score matching and denoising diffusion as an SDE, enabling flexible solvers, controllable generation, and exact likelihood evaluation.
  • 📄
    High-Resolution Image Synthesis with Latent Diffusion Models
    Rombach et al., 2022 — Moves diffusion into an autoencoder latent space, cutting cost by a large factor; the basis of Stable Diffusion and open text-to-image generation.
  • 📄
    Classifier-Free Diffusion Guidance
    Ho & Salimans, 2022 — Removes the need for a separate classifier by jointly training conditional and unconditional models, giving a single dial over the fidelity-diversity trade-off.
  • 📄
    Scalable Diffusion Models with Transformers
    Peebles & Xie, 2023 — Introduces the DiT architecture and shows that sample quality improves predictably with backbone compute, bringing transformer scaling laws to diffusion.
Looking Ahead

Future Directions

  • Few-Step Generation Without Compromise: Closing the remaining gap between distilled one-to-four-step samplers and full multi-step sampling, so that interactive and on-device generation no longer costs diversity or fine structure.
  • Unified Understanding and Generation: Architectures that both interpret and synthesise within one model, letting a system reason about a scene in language and then render or edit it consistently — the direction flow-matching-based multimodal models are heading.
  • Long-Horizon Video and 4D: Maintaining identity, physics, and geometric consistency over minutes rather than seconds, which requires memory mechanisms and 3D-aware representations rather than simply scaling spatiotemporal attention.
  • Provenance, Attribution & Consent: Robust watermarking, training-data attribution, machine unlearning for removal requests, and dataset licensing frameworks — the infrastructure needed for generative vision to operate on a defensible legal footing.
